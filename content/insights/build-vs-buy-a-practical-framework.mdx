Every growing business eventually faces the question: do we build our own software, or buy an existing product? There's no universal answer, but these five questions make the decision much clearer.

## 1. Is this a competitive advantage?

If the capability is what makes you different, such as your pricing engine, your customer experience or your operational edge, building usually wins. If it's a commodity like payroll, email or accounting, buy.

## 2. How well does the off-the-shelf option fit?

If a product covers 80% or more of your needs out of the box, buying is usually faster and cheaper. If you'd need heavy customisation or awkward workarounds, the hidden cost of "buy" grows quickly.

## 3. What's the total cost over three years?

Compare licences, integration, customisation and training against build, hosting and maintenance. Per-seat SaaS pricing that looks cheap today can become expensive as you grow.

## 4. How much control do you need?

Consider data ownership, compliance requirements, roadmap control and vendor risk. If a vendor changes pricing or shuts down a feature, what happens to your business?

## 5. Do you have the capacity to own it?

Custom software needs ongoing care. If you don't have an in-house team, plan for a support partner from day one.

---

**Often the best answer is both:** buy the commodity pieces, and build the thin layer of integration and differentiation on top.
