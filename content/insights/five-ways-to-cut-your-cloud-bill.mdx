Cloud bills rarely jump overnight. They creep up as teams launch resources and forget about them. These five changes usually deliver the biggest savings with the least risk.

## 1. Turn off what nobody uses

Idle development environments, unattached disks, old snapshots and forgotten load balancers add up. Tag every resource with an owner, and schedule non-production environments to shut down outside working hours.

## 2. Right-size your compute

Most instances are provisioned for a peak that never comes. Review CPU and memory utilisation over 30 days and step down where usage is consistently low. Autoscaling handles the spikes.

## 3. Commit to your baseline

If part of your workload runs 24/7, savings plans or committed-use discounts can cut that portion of the bill substantially. Commit only to the steady baseline, and keep the variable part on demand.

## 4. Fix your storage tiers

Logs and backups often sit in the most expensive storage class forever. Lifecycle policies that move older data to cheaper tiers, and eventually delete it, are quick wins.

## 5. Make cost visible

Teams can't optimise what they can't see. Put a cost dashboard in front of every team, broken down by service and environment, and review it in your regular engineering meetings.
